# The Border Collie — A Complete Guide

The Border Collie originates from the border region between England and Scotland. Renowned for its intelligence, agility, and outstanding working ability, it is widely regarded as the most intelligent dog breed in the world.

---

## 1. Quick Facts

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Border Collie |
| Origin | United Kingdom (Anglo-Scottish border) |
| Breed group | Herding / Working dog |
| Lifespan | ~12–15 years |
| Weight | Males ~14–20 kg, females ~12–19 kg |
| Height | Males ~48–56 cm, females ~46–53 cm |
| Intelligence | Ranked #1 among all dog breeds |

---

## 2. Appearance

- **Body**: Medium-sized, slightly longer than tall, well-muscled and athletic — built for both speed and endurance.
- **Coat**: Two types — rough (long) and smooth (short). Double coat: weather-resistant outer layer, insulating undercoat.
- **Colors**: Black-and-white is most common; also blue-and-white, red-and-white, blue (slate), tricolor, and merle.
- **Eyes**: Oval, alert and intensely focused; some individuals have blue or heterochromatic eyes.
- **Ears**: Semi-erect or erect, raised when alert.
- **Tail**: Set low, carried in a curve, often with a white tip.

> The classic Border Collie image is its black-and-white coat and its signature "eye" — the intense stare it uses to control livestock.

---

## 3. Temperament

1. **Highly intelligent** — learns quickly, can understand hundreds of commands.
2. **High energy** — a true working dog that needs plenty of physical and mental stimulation.
3. **Loyal and affectionate** — devoted to its family and eager to cooperate.
4. **Sensitive and alert** — very responsive to its owner's cues and moods.
5. **Strong herding instinct** — may chase and "gather" moving things, including children, other pets, and bicycles.

---

## 4. Exercise & Training

### Exercise
- At least **1–2 hours** of vigorous activity daily (running, frisbee, fetch, agility, etc.).
- Walks alone are not enough; too little exercise leads to anxiety and destructive behavior.

### Training
- Start socialization and basic training as early as possible.
- Border Collies thrive with a "job" — obedience, agility, frisbee, and scent games all help burn energy.
- Use positive reinforcement (treats, toys, praise).

### Environment
- Best suited to homes with a yard or space and owners who can exercise them frequently.
- Apartment living is possible only if daily exercise and companionship are guaranteed.

---

## 5. Grooming

- **Brushing**: Rough coats need brushing 2–3 times a week; increase frequency during shedding season.
- **Bathing**: About once a month or as needed — avoid over-bathing.
- **Ears / teeth / nails**: Clean ears, brush teeth, and trim nails regularly.
- **Diet**: Feed quality food and control portions by weight and activity level to prevent obesity.

---

## 6. Common Health Concerns

Border Collies are generally healthy but watch for these hereditary/common issues:

- Canine hip dysplasia (CHD)
- Eye disorders (Collie Eye Anomaly – CEA, Progressive Retinal Atrophy – PRA)
- Epilepsy
- MDR1 gene mutation (sensitivity to certain drugs)
- Hearing problems (especially in merle individuals)

> Schedule regular check-ups, and when buying or adopting, ask about the parents' health and genetic testing.

---

## 7. Is a Border Collie Right for You?

**Good fit:**
- Active people with plenty of time for companionship
- Owners willing to invest in training and interaction
- Families that enjoy dog sports (agility, frisbee, etc.)

**Not ideal for:**
- People away from home for long hours with little time to spend
- Those who can't guarantee enough daily exercise
- People who want a quiet, low-energy dog

---

## 8. Buying / Adopting a Puppy

- **Reputable source**: Prefer trustworthy breeders or legitimate rescues; avoid sick "puppy-mill" dogs.
- **Meet the parents**: Check their health, temperament, and genetic tests (especially MDR1, CEA).
- **Check alertness**: A healthy puppy is lively, with bright eyes, a moist nose, and a clean, glossy coat.
- **Physical check**: No thinness, no diarrhea, clean and odor-free ears, no limping.
- **Temperament test**: Puppies that approach and explore when gently called are usually friendlier.
- **Paperwork**: Legitimate sources provide vaccination records, deworming logs, and pedigree/health certificates.

> Puppies should ideally stay with their mother until at least **8 weeks** of age.

## 9. Vaccination & Deworming Schedule (Reference)

> Always follow your local veterinarian's advice; brands and regions vary.

### Vaccines

| Age | Vaccine |
| --- | --- |
| 6–8 weeks | First puppy combo vaccine |
| 10–12 weeks | Second combo vaccine |
| 14–16 weeks | Third combo vaccine + rabies |
| Yearly | Combo + rabies booster |

### Deworming

| Type | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Internal | Puppies monthly; adults every 3 months |
| External (ticks/fleas) | Monthly, or per product instructions |

> Until the full puppy vaccine series is complete, avoid crowded or unsanitary places.

---

## 10. Feeding by Life Stage

| Stage | Age | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Puppy | 2–12 months | Puppy formula, 3–4 small meals/day, adequate protein & calcium for bone growth |
| Adolescent | 1–2 years | Transition to adult food, 2 meals/day, plenty of exercise |
| Adult | 2–7 years | Adult food, 1–2 meals/day, control calories by activity to prevent obesity |
| Senior | 7+ years | Senior food, monitor joints, heart, and weight; reduce exercise intensity |

**Notes:**
- Switch foods gradually (over ~7 days) to avoid stomach upset.
- Always provide fresh, clean water.
- Never feed chocolate, onion, garlic, grapes, or xylitol — these are toxic to dogs.

---

## 11. Summary

The Border Collie is intelligent, loyal, and full of energy. It can be a superb working partner and family member, but it demands a lot from its owner — **enough exercise, ongoing training, and dedicated companionship**. Meet those needs and a Border Collie will repay you with intelligence and enthusiasm, becoming your most in-sync companion.
